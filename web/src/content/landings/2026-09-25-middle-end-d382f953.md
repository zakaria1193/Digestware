primer_glossary:
- term: RTL
  definition: Register Transfer Language. An intermediate representation used by GCC
    to represent code during optimization passes. It's a low-level, machine-independent
    language.
- term: MUL_HIGHPART
  definition: An RTL operation representing the multiplication of the high-order part
    of a product. For example, in a 64-bit multiplication, this would be the upper
    32 bits of the 64-bit result.
- term: SMUL_HIGHPART
  definition: Signed MUL_HIGHPART. Represents the high part of a signed multiplication.
- term: UMUL_HIGHPART
  definition: Unsigned MUL_HIGHPART. Represents the high part of an unsigned multiplication.
- term: simplification
  definition: An optimization pass that transforms RTL code into an equivalent but
    simpler form, making it easier for later passes to optimize further or generate
    efficient machine code.
- term: arithmetic right shift
  definition: A bitwise right shift operation that preserves the sign bit of the number.
    If the number is negative, the most significant bit is filled with 1s; otherwise,
    it's filled with 0s.
- term: logical right shift
  definition: A bitwise right shift operation that fills the most significant bit
    with 0s, regardless of the sign of the number.

This patch introduces new RTL simplifications for `SMUL_HIGHPART` and `UMUL_HIGHPART`. The compiler can now recognize that multiplying the high part of a product by zero or one results in zero. Additionally, it optimizes multiplication by powers of two by transforming them into logical or arithmetic right shifts, depending on whether the operation is signed or unsigned. These optimizations improve the efficiency of intermediate representations for multiplication operations.
