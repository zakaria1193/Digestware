When a `std::optional` containing a value is cleared, the destructor of the contained value is invoked. This leaves the underlying union without an active member, which violates core constant expression requirements. To fix this, the commit makes `_M_destroy` activate the dummy union member `_M_empty` after destroying `_M_value`, ensuring that the union always has an active member throughout its lifetime.  The fix uses `std::construct_at` instead of simple assignment to workaround a front end bug.
