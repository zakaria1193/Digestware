GCC's cost model for the `c86-4g-m7` i386 target has been updated to more accurately reflect the true cost of AVX512 512-bit memory operations. Previously, 512-bit loads and stores were assigned costs nearly identical to 256-bit operations, despite the `c86-4g-m7` architecture implementing them as two separate 256-bit transactions. By doubling the cost of 512-bit loads and stores, the vectorizer now correctly prioritizes 32-byte vectorization for dot-product style reduction loops, which can avoid costly reduction epilogues. This change leads to measurable performance improvements in several SPEC benchmarks, notably a 2.32% improvement in SPEC2006 fp_speed.
